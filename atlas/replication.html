Replication of published findings

Taxon explorer · Condition explorer · VANISH / BloSSUM · Replication of published findings · Taxon map · PCA of community profiles · Profile coverage and quality

We took 6,933 published claims that a gut microbe is more or less abundant in people with a condition than in healthy controls, and tried to test each one on this catalog's samples by a plan fixed before the results were seen. 515 of the 1,918 claims that could be tested replicated (26.9 %) in the catalog's own samples; click a condition to see its claims, then a claim to see every project behind it.

Computed on data package 1.34.0 (release R2026.41) and kept as published; it is not recomputed when the catalog moves on.

The catalog's condition labels were revised after this test was run: in release R2026.44 every label was read again and mapped to an ontology term, which moves some samples into or out of a condition's cases. The result above is the registered one and is kept as it was computed on data package 1.34.0; it was not recomputed on the revised labels. The conditions as they now stand are in the Condition explorer.

    How firm is that number?
    • A stricter test. The headline uses the test named in the plan, which is slightly too generous when projects disagree strongly. Under the Hartung-Knapp test, which is very cautious when there are few projects, 191 of 1,918 replicate (10.0 %); read it as the cautious bound.
    • Person identifiers. Using only projects that say which samples come from the same person for at least 90 % of people, 250 of 1,342 replicate (18.6 %); the same 1,342 claims gave 404 in the headline analysis. The drop mixes two things: fewer projects per claim, and the removal of projects in which repeated samples of one person may have been counted as separate people.
    • One condition carries much of it. 562 of the 1,918 testable claims and 255 of the 515 replicated ones are about Parkinson's disease. Without it, 260 of 1,356 replicate (19.2 %); the mean over the 20 conditions with at least 10 testable claims is 22.9 %.
    • Most claims could not be tested. 5,015 of the 6,933 claims had fewer than three independent projects for the condition (3,625) or a taxon too rare in them (1,390). They are neither confirmed nor contradicted.
    • Not replicated is not refuted. Of the 1,403 testable claims that did not replicate, 634 are inconclusive (the data cannot tell a small effect from none), 738 rule out an effect of meaningful size, and 31 point the other way.
    • Chance alone. With the case and control labels shuffled inside every project, 1,000 reruns gave a mean of 0.27 replicated claims, and 24 at the very most, against 515 with the real labels (p = 0.001).
