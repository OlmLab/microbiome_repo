VANISH / BloSSUM

Taxon explorer · Condition explorer · VANISH / BloSSUM · Replication of published findings · Taxon map · PCA of community profiles · Profile coverage and quality

Which gut microbes are commoner in people who live non-industrialized lives, and which in industrialized ones? Of the 464 gut species that could be tested in adults against high-development-index countries, 69 are commoner in non-industrialized people (VANISH) and 37 in industrialized people (BloSSUM). Every tested taxon common enough to draw is a point, placed from more common in non-industrialized people on the left to more common in industrialized people on the right and higher up the more of the samples carry it; the note under the plot says how many are left out as too rarely carried. Hover a point for its numbers, search for a taxon, zoom with the wheel, and click a genus or species to open it in the taxon explorer.

    How to read this plot

    A taxon counts as detected in a sample when the profile assigns it any relative abundance above zero. The smallest non-zero value these profiles record is 0.1 % of a sample's species profile and 0.0009 % of its genus profile. Nothing on this page has been recomputed at a stricter detection threshold.

    The reference group is every quality-checked gut sample from a high-development-index country whose lifestyle is unrecorded or urban-industrialized — 27,662 samples from 477 projects. Public archives are rich in case-control disease projects, so this group holds both healthy people and patients: 12,787 of those samples (46 %) carry a recorded health condition other than healthy control, 7,999 are recorded as healthy controls and 6,876 carry no value at all. They are not separated here.

    What the two names do and do not mean, what the non-industrialized group pools, and what this plot cannot show are in the sections below.

    Where the terms come from

    Both terms are defined in Carter et al. 2023, Cell 186:3111-3124.e13 (10.1016/j.cell.2023.05.046): BloSSUM is “Bloom or Selected in Societies of Urbanization/Modernization”, VANISH “Volatile and/or Associated Negatively with Industrialized Societies of Humans”. Earlier uses may exist; no published definition before this one was found.

    The one quantitative definition found is Carter's: a Fisher's exact test of prevalence in Hadza against industrial samples with a 95th-percentile cut, giving 124 VANISH and 63 BloSSUM genomes in GTDB r95.

    The groups, and where their labels come from

    The non-industrialized group takes samples whose stated lifestyle in the catalog is hunter-gatherer, indigenous community, pastoralist or traditional agriculturalist, plus samples labelled H-G or Transitional in Carter et al. 2023 Supplemental Table S4. Those Carter labels are external labels, not catalog values; the table below counts them separately.

    The non-industrialized group pools several ways of living. Its 781 adult samples divide by the lifestyle value they carry in the catalog into 288 indigenous community, 183 rural non-industrialized, 120 no value recorded (external label only), 82 traditional agriculturalist, 80 hunter-gatherer, 14 other lifestyle and 14 pastoralist, and they come from 16 projects in 13 countries. These ways of living differ from one another as well as from industrialized populations, so part of a taxon's score may reflect the shift to agriculture rather than industrialization. The comparison that takes stated catalog labels only (464 samples, 10 projects, in the sensitivity table below), is the narrower comparison.

    Both rules in plain words

    Model rule. For each taxon, a logistic model of being detected, with the group, the sequencing depth and a random effect for the project, so that one large project cannot carry the result. The score is the group term in log2 odds. The p-value comes from shuffling the group label between projects (200 shuffles for the primary contrasts), q is Benjamini-Hochberg, and a taxon is classed only if it survives leaving any one project out: q < 0.05, |score| ≥ 2.0, a difference of at least 10 percentage points between the groups' mean per-project prevalences, and p ≤ 0.01 in every refit.

    VANISH and BloSSUM here are names for a difference in how often a taxon is detected, not for absence. Every one of the 69 VANISH species on this page is detected in the industrialized reference group — in a median of 542 of the 27,662 reference samples, and in a median of 200 of the 477 reference projects; the rarest is detected in 3 of the 27,662 reference samples. The 37 BloSSUM species are likewise all detected in the non-industrialized group, in a median of 56 of its 781 samples.

    Simple rule. No lifestyle labels at all: each person is placed by the HDI of their country (industrialized ≥ 0.80, non-industrialized < 0.70, transitional in between and not tested), a taxon counts as carried if it is detected in any of that person's samples, and the two bands are compared with Fisher's exact test. VANISH = carried by at least 20 % of non-industrialized and at most 5 % of industrialized people, q < 0.05, in at least two countries; BloSSUM is the mirror image.

    The simple rule compares people by the development index of their country with Fisher's exact test. It makes no adjustment for sequencing depth and does not account for which project a person came from; the model rule does both (a depth covariate, a project random effect, project-label permutations and a leave-one-project-out check). Where the two rules disagree, the model rule is the one to trust.

    Every result on this page uses the country HDI table of the package it was computed on. Three territories without a UNDP value take one from another published source, recorded in that table (Taiwan, Puerto Rico, French Guiana).

    What a “StIL candidate” is, and what it is not

    StIL — “stable independent of lifestyle” — is a third class named in Carter, Liu, Olm et al., Nature 658 (2026) (10.1038/s41586-026-11106-1): species that are prevalent across lifestyles and whose strains are nevertheless isolated between two distant non-industrialized populations. That paper gives no prevalence threshold and publishes no per-species list, and the strain half needs assembled genomes, which these read-based profiles do not carry. The preprint version is 10.1101/2025.08.15.670570.

    What is marked here is therefore only the prevalence half, with our own threshold, and it is called a candidate everywhere: a taxon carried by at least 20 % of both groups, not VANISH and not BloSSUM, whose difference between the groups is smaller than two-fold — under the model rule the score interval (score ± 1.96 standard errors) stays inside ±1.0 and stays there when any one project is left out; under the simple rule |lfc| < 1.0, in at least two countries. It says that a difference of that size is excluded, not that something was discovered: with shuffled labels many common taxa would qualify, by design, and no p-value is attached. Under the model rule, for adults against high-HDI countries, 7 species, 13 genera and 1 family are StIL candidates. Under the model rule the class is worked out for adults only.

    Does the class rule invent classes? (shuffled-label check)

    The whole pipeline was rerun on labels shuffled between projects, species level, adults. No shuffle produced a single candidate or class, over 40 shuffles.

    Limits

    The non-industrialized group rests on few independent projects, and one project contributes much of it; leaving one project out at a time is the guard. Sandpiper assigns reads to GTDB genomes, so a taxon with no reference genome is invisible, and non-industrialized communities are the less well sequenced ones. About half the catalog has no profile at all. Lifestyle labels are read from project records and are incomplete; the external labels come from one paper's table. This page shows no result by health condition. One further limit was found after this page was published: the step that keeps one sample per person compares the person identifier across projects, although it is unique only within a project (3,023 of these identifiers occur in more than one project group, on 31,671 of the 231,657 adult gut samples of the package this page was computed on), so the published run dropped or swapped some samples of different people who happen to share one; how much that moves the classes shown here is being measured.

    All results are statements about taxa that GTDB R232 contains and that SingleM's marker genes can see: 24,324 species appear in these gut profiles at all, and 464 of them met the testing rule of the model-based analysis for adults against high-HDI countries. They are not statements about strains, about functions, or about lineages that no reference catalogue holds.

    Robustness — three exploratory checks of the groups behind the rule: by lifestyle, by project, by sequencing depth (not pre-specified; no class on this page depends on them)

    These three checks were run after the classes were fixed, in answer to a published critique of how VANISH and BloSSUM lists are made and read (Ibata et al., bioRxiv 2026.11.17.767315, 10.64898/2026.11.17.767315; a preprint, not peer reviewed). They are exploratory: they were not pre-specified, no threshold and no class was changed for them, and no taxon on this page is classed or unclassed by what they say. They are reported as they came out, including where a class does not hold.

    1. Each classed taxon's prevalence, split by lifestyle

    The non-industrialized group of the primary contrast (781 adult samples) was split by the lifestyle value its samples carry in the catalog; samples that are in the group only through Carter's external label, with no catalog value, form a stratum of their own. A taxon holds in a lifestyle when its prevalence there is still at least 10 percentage points on the expected side of the reference group's mean per-project prevalence — the margin the class rule itself uses — and for a StIL candidate when both sides stay at or above 20 %. A lifestyle with fewer than 10 samples is not judged. At species level 10 of the 69 VANISH species hold in all 7 lifestyles and the median VANISH species holds in 4; none fails in every lifestyle. 13 of the 37 BloSSUM species hold in all of them. At genus level it is 19 of 114 and 22 of 48; at family level 4 of 28 and 3 of 7. The smallest two lifestyles hold 14 and 14 samples, so prevalence there moves in steps of several percentage points and a taxon failing in them is weak evidence.

    2. The simple rule, stratified by project

    The simple rule's single 2×2 table of people was rebuilt project by project and recombined with a Cochran–Mantel–Haenszel test, the standard way of keeping a comparison inside each project. A project can contribute only if it holds people of both development bands, and only 7 of the 655 projects that contribute adult people do (997 of 87,730 people, 1.1 %). That is the first thing this check shows: the industrialized and non-industrialized people come from different projects, so the pooled comparison is a comparison between projects, and a project-stratified test of it can use about one person in a hundred. Where the test can be computed it keeps the direction of the pooled comparison for 22 of the 22 VANISH species and reaches p < 0.05 for 22 of them. Because so few people are left, the figure beside it carries more: for a VANISH species the one project that contributes the most non-industrialized carriers contributes a median 41 % of them, and with that project left out 12 of the 22 VANISH species still meet the rule's prevalence thresholds and 10 do not.

    3. The simple rule, inside strata of sequencing depth

    Adults were put into 5 equal-sized strata by the total root coverage of their samples — a measure of how deeply they were sequenced — and the rule was run again inside each stratum. The two bands are not far apart to begin with: the median non-industrialized adult person has 1,506 of root coverage against 1,280 for the median industrialized one. The classes nevertheless move with depth. Averaged over the VANISH species, carriage in the non-industrialized band rises from 13 % in the shallowest fifth to 32 % in the middle fifth, and carriage in the industrialized band from 1.4 % in the shallowest to 3.7 % in the deepest. So in the shallowest fifth 20 of the 22 VANISH species fall below the rule's 20 % floor in the non-industrialized band, and in the deepest fifth 4 cross its 5 % ceiling in the industrialized one. 2 of the 22 VANISH species meet the rule in all 5 depth strata, and the median meets it in 4. At genus level 0 of 34 meet it in all five (median 3). For infants the median VANISH species meets it in 1 of 5. What fails is the prevalence threshold itself, not the test: the counts above are the same whether or not Fisher's test inside the stratum is required to clear q < 0.05.
