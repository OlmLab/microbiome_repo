Methods

About › Methods · see also Scope, Sources & acknowledgements, Fields.

Two tiers, one rule: every value on this site points back to a record in a public archive or an open paper.

1 · Registry: enumeration and classification

Every INSDC BioProject with METAGENOMIC / WGS-type runs — deposited in ENA, NCBI SRA or DDBJ and enumerated through ENA's mirror (portal API) in dated batches — is a registry study; the enumeration reaches every shotgun-metagenome study, not only human ones, and each is then classified for host (completeness is checked for every batch). Per study the questions are: host (human / non-human / mixed / unknown), body sites from a controlled vocabulary anchored to UBERON (17 terms), life stages, population flags, assay (shotgun / amplicon / genome / transcriptome / other) and access. Deterministic rules decide what the archive fields settle (host taxon, library fields, numeric age with unit); the rest is classified by two independent model readings of title, description and sample attributes, with disagreements settled by a third, more capable reading; every call carries its evidence rows. 57,241 of 64,369 studies are classified (89 %); 7,128 are pending. The sample tier harvests BioSample attributes for human studies and normalises site, age, sex and country from them (637,397 BioSamples in 4,381 studies).

2 · Catalog scope

A project enters the catalog when the scope classification of its archive record, sample attributes, read check and linked paper finds human-associated communities sequenced by shotgun DNA or RNA sequencing, at any body site (Scope lists what is in and out). Every BioSample of such a project with at least one in-scope data set is a catalog sample; amplicon and single-cell runs are left out run by run. Each sample keeps its own body-site code, and each run is labelled DNA or RNA. Age categories (neonate, infant, child, adolescent, adult, elderly) come from the numeric age when known, else from the sample's stated life stage, a cohort-wide statement, or the study's primary life stage — the basis is recorded per sample.

4 · Evidence and confidence

Every value carries the source it was read from, a locator (attribute key, PMCID, PMID or accession), the quoted text (≤ 12 words, verbatim), what produced it and a confidence tier. Confidence is a fixed tier per kind of source, not a calibrated probability (Fields › Confidence); the exact model is recorded on every model-derived value. The columns that hold all of this are in the data dictionary.

5 · Cohorts, papers, authors

Papers are linked to studies from Europe PMC (accession mentions, data-availability statements) and NCBI BioProject publication records, with a relation (own_data when the paper describes the deposit, unsure when it may re-analyse it). Authors are the Europe PMC author lists of those papers, string-matched and not disambiguated. Cohorts group studies that share an own-data paper or a curated cohort record (connected components); the grouping is recomputed at every release.

6 · Releases and provenance

Releases are immutable and numbered (R2026.n); each maps to a data-package semver and to Git tags in the data and site repositories. Fact tables carry release_added / release_retired / package_added; the changes pages list what each release added. The site is generated deterministically from the package (byte-identical rebuilds); data/manifest.json lists every data file with its SHA-256.

7 · Limitations

Why some values are still missing. Every project went through the sources we can reach: the archive records (all BioSample attributes; sample names, titles and library names decoded per project by two independent model readings that must agree), the abstract and archive description, the open full text (methods prose and article tables), the open supplementary tables, and external curated resources. Projects without a linked paper were searched again by accession and title. Supplementary files come from Europe PMC; supplements that could not be downloaded are marked not available below and on the Contribute page, so they can be shared or retried. Each project page shows the same checkmarks for that project, and the Projects table can be filtered by source. How many values each source contributes, overall and per field, is on Where the values come from.

  • Coverage is bounded by what submitters deposited and what open papers state: 24 % of samples have an age, 45 % a health-condition code, 31 % a sex, 14 % an antibiotic-exposure value.
  • Case–control cohorts stay unknown at sample level unless an attribute or table names the group; cohort-wide codes are assigned only when every sequenced person shares the condition.
  • Archive ages without a unit are read as years for projects outside the in-depth curation; ambiguous or conflicting values are not committed.
  • PDF-only supplements are not parsed, and papers that are not open access contribute only their abstract.
  • Values added later fill gaps only: they never replace a value from an equal or higher-ranked source, and where both exist they agree for sex, country and age in ≥ 98 % of compared samples.
  • When two sample-level sources give incompatible values for the same sample — different sex or country, antibiotics yes against no, ages more than 30 days and 20 % apart, BMI more than 2 apart, or healthy control against a disease — the catalog publishes neither: the field is left empty and both sides, with their sources and quotes, are kept in the package. Differences of formatting, rounding or specificity (Crohn's disease against IBD) are not contradictions; precedence decides those.
  • Registry classification of pending studies and the model-based calls carry their evidence but are not human-reviewed; use the Flag an issue link on any study or cohort page.
  • Sandpiper community profiles are shown where they exist; the registry records how many runs of each study are in the Sandpiper snapshot.
