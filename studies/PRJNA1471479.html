Archive description

Diabetic foot ulcers are a major complication of type 2 diabetes mellitus, but whether the gut microbiome captures systemic microbial features associated with advanced ulcer severity remains unclear. We performed shotgun metagenomic sequencing of stool samples from 43 patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus and active diabetic foot ulcers classified as Wagner grades 1-4. Patients with Wagner grades 1-3 were compared with patients with Wagner grade 4 gangrenous disease. De novo assembly and binning recovered 440 dereplicated metagenome-assembled genomes meeting medium-quality or high-quality thresholds. Community-level diversity and dominant-taxon composition did not clearly separate Wagner grade 4 from Wagner grades 1-3, whereas genome-level feature analysis identified a microbial signature associated with advanced disease. Random forest stability selection yielded 24 informative metagenome-assembled genomes, and intersection with covariate-adjusted MaAsLin2 analysis prioritized three shared genomes: one Escherichia coli genome enriched in Wagner grade 4 and two Collinsella genomes depleted in Wagner grade 4. Functional annotation suggested that the Wagner grade 4-enriched Escherichia coli genome carried antibiotic-resistance and virulence-factor signals and encoded respiratory metabolic capacity, whereas the two Collinsella genomes lacked detectable resistance and virulence hits and showed metabolically compact profiles. These findings support longitudinal gut metagenomic validation to determine whether this microbial signal tracks diabetic foot ulcer progression, treatment response, or recovery.
