Archive description

Over the past decade, accumulating evidence pointed to the clinical impact of the</p><p>intestinal microbiota on immunotherapeutic outcomes across various cancers. Although</p><p>specific gut microbial species have been associated with beneficial responses in meta-</p><p>analyses, no consensus exists on a gut microbiome signature for personalized prediction</p><p>of immunoresistance in clinical practice. Based on fecal shotgun metagenomics (MG)</p><p>sequencing data from a retrospective discovery cohort of 245 patients with advanced non-</p><p>small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) treated with immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICI), we</p><p>constructed species-level co-abundance networks that were clustered into species</p><p>interacting groups (SIG) correlating with overall survival. Thirty-seven and forty-five</p><p>metagenomic species (MGS) belonged to clusters associated with resistance (SIG1) and</p><p>response (SIG2) to ICI, respectively. For each patient, we calculated the SIG1-SIG2 ratio</p><p>and integrated the trichotomic relative abundance of Akkermansia muciniphila (Akk)</p><p>when this ratio was poorly discriminant, to obtain a final TOPOSCORE, associated</p><p>with clinical outcome in a validation cohort of 254 NSCLC patients and in a third cohort</p><p>of 216 renal and urothelial carcinoma amenable to ICI. The TOPOSCORE outperformed</p><p>PD-L1 tumor scoring. Finally, the 83 MGS-based TOPOSCORE was translated into a 21</p><p>bacterial probe set qPCR-based scoring that was validated in a prospective cohort of</p><p>NSCLC. Custom scoring of intestinal bacteria by the TOPOSCORE could represent a</p><p>dynamic diagnosis tool of gut dysbiosis, that stratifies patients for microbiota-related</p><p>interventions.
