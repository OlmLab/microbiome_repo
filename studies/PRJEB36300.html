Archive description

Acute infection in pancreatic and biliary tracts are often recognized to result from bacterial translocation from the gastrointestinal tract. However, little is known about the gut microbial content in patients suffering from such diseases. Here, we cross-sectionally characterized the gut microbiota of 82 acute pancreatitis (AP) patients, 47 acute biliary tract infection (AC) patients and 173 matched healthy controls using whole-metagenome shotgun sequencing. The gut microbiome of patients with AP and AC exhibited reduced intrasample diversity, alteration of gut microbial profiles, disturbance of microbial interaction networks, disruption of microbial function and antibiotic resistome as compared with that of healthy subjects. Microbial signatures of AP and AC were highly consistent: they both showed a marked over-representation of pathobionts including Escherichia coli, Parabacteroides merdae, Enterococcus faecium and Klebsiella pneumoniae, depleted Bifidobacterium longum and some short-chain fatty acids-producing species. Random forest model trained from the AP-associated species yielded high performance for distinguishing both AP and AC from the healthy controls. Our data and results present the landscape of gut microbiome alterations in AP and AC, and suggest that gut microbiota is a potential target for diagnosis and intervention of pancreatic and biliary tract infections.
