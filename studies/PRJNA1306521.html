Archive description

As global life expectancy continues to rise, identifying contributors to healthy ageing has become increasingly important. Blue Zones, which are regions with exceptional longevity, serve as an optimal setting for investigation of age-related health determinants, such as the gut microbiome (GM). In this case-control study, we analyzed fecal samples from 92 participants using shotgun metagenomic sequencing to compare microbial diversity, taxonomic composition, functional gene profiles and strain-level variation in the GM of long-living adults (>=85 years) and young children (3-5 years) from the Greek Blue Zone island of Ikaria and counterparts from the nearby non-Blue Zone island of Astypalaia, aiming to identify features potentially associated with longevity.</p><p>While children showed no significant GM differences between the two islands, Blue Zone adults exhibited distinct GM features compared to their counterparts, including higher levels of Bacteroides dorei and Bacteroides fragilis, and lower levels of Escherichia coli and other Enterobacteriaceae. Regarding microbial functional pathways, Blue Zone adults demonstrated an enhanced carbohydrate-degradation capacity, and a reduced abundance of genes involved in ABC transporters, two-component systems, microbial metabolism in diverse environments, and biosynthesis of secondary metabolites. Notably, no strain-level differences among prominent bacterial species were observed between the two groups, suggesting that the detected functional differences arise at the community level rather than from within-species variation.</p><p>These findings suggest that the GM of Blue Zone adults differs taxonomically and functionally from that of adults on the control island, with features potentially linked to healthy ageing that appear to develop gradually during adulthood.
