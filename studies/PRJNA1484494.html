Details
Archive description

Autism spectrum disorder (ASD) is a neurodevelopmental disorder characterized by impairments in social communication and interaction, along with restricted and repetitive behaviors. Gastrointestinal dysfunction and gut microbiota dysbiosis are commonly observed in children with ASD, suggesting a potential role of the gut-brain axis in the pathogenesis of this disorder. Fecal microbiota transplantation (FMT) and ketogenic diet (KD) are two emerging interventions that target the gut microbiota, yet their comparative effects on clinical symptoms and gut microbial composition remain poorly understood.</p><p>This study enrolled children diagnosed with ASD according to DSM-5 criteria and the Autism Diagnostic Observation Schedule (ADOS). Participants were assigned to either FMT or KD intervention groups. Clinical outcomes were evaluated using the Childhood Autism Rating Scale (CARS) and the Aberrant Behavior Checklist (ABC) at baseline and at the end of the intervention period. Fecal samples were collected at corresponding time points for shotgun metagenomic sequencing on the Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform.</p><p>The primary objectives of this study are: (1) to compare the clinical efficacy of FMT and KD in improving core ASD symptoms (2) to characterize the gut microbiota composition and functional gene profiles associated with each intervention using shotgun metagenomic sequencing.</p><p>The raw sequencing data generated from this study are deposited in the NCBI Sequence Read Archive (SRA) to facilitate open data sharing and to support future meta-analyses and mechanistic investigations into microbiota-targeted interventions for ASD. This dataset provides valuable resources for researchers interested in the gut-brain axis, microbial therapeutics, and the pathophysiology of autism spectrum disorder.
