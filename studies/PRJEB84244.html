Archive description

Background: High-altitude mountaineering presents challenges like hypoxia, extreme temperatures, and limited food availability, potentially affecting athletes’ health, performance, and the gut microbiome - a key mediator of nutrient metabolism and immune function. Objectives: This study examined how high-altitude exposure and expedition-specific dietary changes influence gut microbiome composition, functional pathways, and their relationships with performance and health markers in experienced mountaineers. Methods: Seventeen Polish male mountaineers (23–40 years old) participating in multi-week expeditions above 3000 masl were assessed before and after their climbs. Assessments included dietary intake analysis, blood and urine biomarkers, aerobic and anaerobic performance tests (VO₂max and Wingate tests), and metagenomic sequencing of the gut microbiome. Bioinformatic and statistical analyses evaluated changes in microbiome composition and function and their correlations with physiological and dietary parameters. Results: High-altitude exposure was associated with significant shifts in gut microbial composition and functional capacity. While the total number of bacterial species and functions remained stable, the glucose degradation pathway increased post-expedition. Participants with greater microbiome shifts showed improved performance (e.g., higher VT₂ pace) and had richer baseline microbiomes. Pre-expedition, certain microbial functions were associated with vitamin B₆ and C intake, while post-expedition correlations involved specific macronutrients and micronutrients. Additionally, some microbiome changes correlated with blood markers, indicating links to nutrient metabolism and electrolyte balance. Conclusions: The gut microbiome of high-altitude mountaineers adapts to extreme environmental stress and dietary changes, influencing metabolic, immune, and performance-related processes. Optimizing dietary strategies to support a beneficial microbiome profile may enhance resilience and performance in challenging high-altitude environments.
