A low-digestibility protein supplementation during an energy-restricted diet reduces visceral fat and stimulates gut microbiota aminoacid metabolism in subjects with metabolic syndrome

Archive description

Objective Interactions between diet and gut microbiota are critical for metabolic health. We investifated the effects of a low-digestibility protein supplementation alongside a mild caloric restriction on visceral fat and on the gut microbiota. Design Twelve week-long randomised double blind controlled dietary intervention study in individuals with metabolic syndrome. Particpants were randomised to receive a low-digestibility protein supplementation (milk-derived proteins, Intervention Product, IP) or an isocaloric normoproteic comparator (pea derived proteins, Comparator Product, CP) alongside a mild enery restriction. Visceral fat mass loss was assessed with CT scan. Gut microbiota profiles from shotgun sequencing were explored in a patient subset at the compositional and functional levels as well as by using in vitro fermenter experiments.Results Visceral fat decreased in both groups (IP group: -20.8 ± 23.2 cm2 CP group: -14.5 ± 24.3 cm2) with a greater reduction (p<0.05) with the IP supplementation in the Per Protocole population (Full Analysis Set population: p=0.09). In both groups, the magnitude of weight, fat mass and visceral fat mass loss during the intervention was associated with a gain in microbial diversity (p<0.05). Comparing gut microbiota taxonomy in IP vs. CP showed little changes in bacteria composition but major differences were seen at the functional level. IP (vs. CP) significantly induced bacterial amino acid metabolism with increases in both amino acid synthesis and degradation. Conclusion A low-digestibility protein supplementation alongside a mild energy restriction leads to higher visceral fat mass loss and to an activation of gut microbiota amino-acid metabolism.
