Archive description

The prevalence of obesity in South Korea has been steadily rising, especially in rural regions such as Gangwon Province, which has a relatively older population and higher obesity rates. This study aimed to identify gut microbiome features associated with metabolic health in Gangwon residents using clinical, dietary, and microbiome data from 180 adult women in the GOMS cohort. Fecal samples were analyzed whole metagenome shotgun sequencing and qPCR to assess taxonomic and functional microbiota profiles. Participants were categorized into high- and low-risk metabolic groups based on seven clinical domains, including obesity, insulin sensitivity, liver and kidney function, lipids, blood pressure, and cardiovascular markers. 15 bacterial species differed significantly between groups, with Anaerostipes hadrus enriched in the low-risk group. Additionally, 22 microbial metabolic pathways varied, including butyrate-related pathways enriched in the low-risk group and palmitic acid-related pathways elevated in the high-risk group. LPS modification pathways also showed greater enrichment in the high-risk group, even among individuals with similar obesity levels. These findings suggest that metabolic phenotypes can be stratified by clinical markers and are linked to distinct gut microbiome profiles, highlighting potential microbial contributions to host metabolic regulation
