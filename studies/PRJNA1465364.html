Details
Archive description

Gut microbial production of short chain fatty acids (SCFAs) is critical for intestinal and metabolic health. This double blind, randomized, placebo controlled trial examined whether colon delivered vitamin C modulates gut microbial metabolism in healthy adults aged 50 to 70 years. Participants (n = 264) received 80, 200, or 500 mg per day vitamin C, or placebo, for 12 weeks. The primary outcome was fecal SCFA concentrations, with secondary outcomes including gut microbiota composition, fecal redox potential and pH, intestinal health markers, and gastrointestinal tolerance. Supplementation with 500 mg per day vitamin C significantly increased total fecal SCFAs compared with placebo, driven mainly by acetate. Participants with low baseline SCFA levels showed within group increases at 80 mg and 500 mg doses. The highest dose was also associated with a reduced fecal redox potential that correlated inversely with SCFA concentrations. Microbiome diversity was unchanged, although dose specific taxonomic shifts were observed. All doses were well tolerated. These results show that vitamin C can enhance gut microbial metabolic activity without major changes in microbiome structure.
