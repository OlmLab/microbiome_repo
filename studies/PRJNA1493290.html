Archive description

This BioProject contains whole-genome shotgun metagenomic sequencing data generated from fecal samples collected during a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, parallel-group clinical trial investigating the effects of heat-killed postbiotic interventions on the human gut microbiome in overweight adults with mild-to-moderate gastrointestinal discomfort.</p><p>The study was designed to evaluate the effects of two heat-killed postbiotic formulations, Enterococcus faecalis EF-2001 and Lactobacillus plantarum beLP1, compared with placebo. Participants were randomly assigned to one of the study arms and received the assigned intervention according to the clinical trial protocol. Stool samples were collected at predefined time points before and after the intervention for gut microbiome analysis.</p><p>The human gut microbiome plays an essential role in gastrointestinal, metabolic, and immune homeostasis. Alterations in microbial community composition and function have been associated with obesity, gastrointestinal disorders, and other chronic health conditions. Heat-killed microbial preparations (postbiotics) have emerged as promising nutritional interventions that may beneficially modulate the gut microbiome while providing improved stability and safety compared with live probiotics.</p><p>Microbial DNA was extracted from fecal samples and analyzed using whole-genome shotgun metagenomic sequencing on an Illumina sequencing platform. Raw paired-end sequencing reads were generated to enable comprehensive characterization of the gut microbial community, including taxonomic composition, microbial diversity, functional gene content, metabolic pathway profiling, and comparative analyses of microbiome responses to the postbiotic interventions.</p><p>This BioProject contains de-identified raw sequencing data and associated BioSample metadata for all study samples. Human participant data were collected following approval by the appropriate Institutional Review Board (IRB) or Independent Ethics Committee (IEC), and written informed consent was obtained from all participants before enrollment. All samples were anonymized prior to public submission in accordance with applicable ethical and privacy requirements.</p><p>The dataset is being made publicly available to support research on the human gut microbiome, postbiotic interventions, microbiome-host interactions, comparative metagenomics, and the development of bioinformatics methods for shotgun metagenomic analyses. These data provide a valuable resource for future studies investigating the impact of postbiotic supplementation on gut microbial composition and functional potential in overweight adults.
