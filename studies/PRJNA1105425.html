Archive description

Understanding the cause vs consequence relationship of gut inflammation and microbial dysbiosis in inflammatory bowel diseases (IBD) requires a reproducible mouse model of human-microbiota-driven experimental colitis. We previously showed using 16S-Seq analysis that human fecal microbiota transplant (FMT) transfer efficiency is an underappreciated source of experimental variability in human microbiota associated (HMA) mice. Pooled human IBD patient fecal microbiota engrafted germ-free (GF) mice with low amplicon sequence variant (ASV)-level transfer efficiency, resulting in high recipient-to-recipient variation of microbiota composition and colitis severity in HMA Il-10-/- mice. In contrast, mouse-to-mouse transfer of mouse-adapted human IBD patient microbiota transferred with high efficiency and low compositional variability resulting in highly consistent and reproducible colitis phenotypes in recipient Il-10-/- mice. Human-to-mouse FMT caused a population bottleneck with reassembly of microbiota composition that was host inflammatory environment specific. Mouse-adaptation in the inflamed Il-10-/-host reassembled a more aggressive microbiota that induced more severe colitis in serial transplant to Il-10-/- mice than the distinct microbiota reassembled in non-inflamed WT hosts. Using shot-gun whole metagenome sequencing, we extended this analysis, demonstrating that gene family pathways transfer at universally high levels due to functional redundancy between microbes. However, relatively lower gene family pathway transfer efficiency was still found for human- to-mouse transfer compared to mouse-to-mouse transfer of mouse-adapted human IBD patient microbiota. The host inflammatory environment governed stability of the engrafted microbiome, measured by taxonomic variation and gene family pathway variation. Methods: Germ-free, male and female, 129SvEv strain WT (129.WT) and Il10-/- (129.IL10KO) mice were colonized with pooled human IBD patient fecal microbiota. At 28 days post-colonization, mouse fecal pellets were collected and subject to DNA extraction followed by 2x150 paired end shotgun whole metagenome sequencing (WGS) on an Illumina NovaSeq X. Fecal pellets from these human microbiota associated mice were also collected between 14-21 days post-colonization, anaerobically homogenized to a fecal slurry (mouse-adapted human IBD fecal microbiome), and transplanted to germ-free, male and female, 129SvEv strain WT (129.WT) and Il10-/- (129.IL10KO) mice.
